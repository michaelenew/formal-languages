# 0003 — Is there a basis whose generators are invertible everywhere? No, twice; and what the honest positive answer is

Question: can the calculator basis be generated by functions that are
bijections on their whole domain, so that no inverse is multivalued and
no branch cut appears?

## 1. Two independent obstructions, both theorems

**Totality.** A function invertible everywhere is in particular defined
everywhere. A word (composition) of total functions is total. But the
calculator basis contains partial functions: x/y at y = 0, ln at 0, and
over ℂ also 1/x. So the generators cannot all be total, whatever the
domain (ℝ, ℂ, or the Riemann sphere, where 1/x is total but exp is not).

**Single-valuedness.** A composition of single-valued functions is
single-valued on its domain. ln and √ are not single-valued on any
punctured neighbourhood of 0. So some generator must itself carry a
branch point (0001 §2b condition 3). The branch cut is not an artifact
of EML; every basis has one somewhere. EML puts all of it into Log, and
its subtraction keeps the resulting defect to one ray (0001 §5).

Over ℂ there is a third, sharper fact: an injective entire function is
affine (az + b), and an injective meromorphic function on the sphere is
a Möbius map. Neither generates anything transcendental. So over ℂ no
transcendental generator is injective at all; non-injectivity is forced,
and for exp it takes the specific form ker(exp) = 2πiℤ — the same object
as the Gödel door of 0002. **Invertibility fails exactly at the
integers.**

## 2. What can be done over ℝ: a Latin-square operator

Over the reals, total bijections with elementary inverses exist: sinh
and asinh, odd powers and odd roots. The packaging lemma with φ = sinh
gives

    F(x, y) = sinh(x) − asinh(y),

which is a bijection of ℝ in each slot separately (checked: strictly
monotone and onto in x for fixed y, and in y for fixed x). It is a
quasigroup operation on ℝ, the continuous analogue of a Latin square.
Real EML is not (x ↦ e^x − ln y is not onto ℝ).

With the constant c = asinh(1/2) — chosen so that 2 sinh c = 1 — the
closure reaches 0 at depth 3 (word checked), hence + and − by the lemma,
and then exp exactly:

    e^x  = sinh x + sinh(x + c) − sinh(x − c)          (checked, 1000 points)
    e^−x = sinh(x + c) − sinh(x − c) − sinh x

(from sinh(x + c) − sinh(x − c) = 2 cosh x sinh c = cosh x.) So the
everywhere-invertible real basis generates the additive group, sinh,
asinh, and both exponentials. By §1 it provably never generates ln, 1/x
or x/y: every one of its words is a total real-analytic function of its
arguments. Whether it generates x·y (which is total) is open here; the
depth-4 search over real points did not find it.

## 3. The positive answer: invertible on the Riemann surface

exp: ℂ → ℂ* is not injective, but exp: ℂ → (Riemann surface of log) is a
bijection, with ln as its exact inverse. On that surface every EML
identity holds at every point, with no cut and no exception. The cut
appears only when the surface is projected back down to ℂ by choosing a
principal branch. This is precisely the "identity of germs" convention
of 0001 §5, now stated positively: **EML is everywhere invertible on the
universal cover; the branch cut is the price of insisting that values
live in ℂ.** For a symbolic system (rewriting, Risch, convexity) the
surface is the natural home and nothing is lost; for a calculator that
must return one complex number, something must be lost, by §1.

## Summary

| domain | everywhere-invertible generators | what they can generate | what they cannot |
|---|---|---|---|
| ℂ | affine / Möbius only | rational degree-1 maps | anything transcendental |
| ℝ | sinh, asinh, odd roots, ... | +, −, sinh, asinh, e^x, e^−x | ln, 1/x, x/y (totality) |
| log surface | exp and ln, mutually inverse | everything, exactly | nothing; the cut is a projection artifact |
