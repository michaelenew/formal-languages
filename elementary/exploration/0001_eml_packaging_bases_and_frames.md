# 0001 — EML: why one operator suffices, what other bases exist, the eigen-frames, and convex integration

Machine checks: `output/eml_bases.py` (run directly). Access note: arXiv
and the discussion pages are blocked from this sandbox, so the paper and
its follow-ups were read only through abstracts and secondary summaries;
every formula below is derived here and checked here, not copied.

## 0. The result, as stated, and its published neighbours

- **Odrzywołek (arXiv 2603.21852, Mar 2026).** `eml(x, y) = exp(x) − Log(y)`
  with the constant 1 generates a 36-symbol "scientific calculator" basis:
  +, −, ×, ÷, powers, roots, exp, ln, trig, hyperbolic, e, π, i. Every
  expression is a binary tree of identical nodes: `S → 1 | eml(S, S)`.
  Found by exhaustive search (reported as ~6 million candidates); e.g.
  `ln x = eml(1, eml(eml(1, x), 1))`. Also used as a trainable circuit for
  symbolic regression.
- **Stachowiak (2604.23893).** The operator carries two ingredients, an
  abelian group and a functional inverse; that structure yields
  generalisations. (§1 below is my own derivation of exactly this.)
- **Carney (2605.01636).** Variable-free EML trees are Chow's EL numbers;
  every such number is computable; Chaitin's Ω is inexpressible.
- **Germany–Abdo–Bakarji (2606.23179).** Parametrised EML trees are
  universal approximators on Sobolev classes.
- **stylewarning (blog).** Under the classical (Liouville/Ritt) definition,
  elementary functions include roots of polynomials; a branch of the root
  of the generic quintic has monodromy S₅, while EML terms have solvable
  monodromy, so it is not EML-expressible. The paper's theorem is about
  its own 36-symbol basis, which has no general root-of-polynomial symbol.

So the precise class EML generates is the **exp-log (EL) class**: closure
of the rationals / rational functions under field operations, exp and
Log. Not the full Ritt class. Everything below is about the EL class.

## 1. Why it works: a packaging lemma

**Lemma (packaging).** Let (G, ⋆) be an abelian group with identity e_G
and inverse inv, and φ: G → G a bijection. Put

    F(x, y) := φ(x) ⋆ inv(φ⁻¹(y)),      k := φ(e_G).

Then the clone generated by F and the constant k contains φ, φ⁻¹, ⋆,
inv, e_G and φ(k):

    φ(x)      = F(x, k)                          since φ⁻¹(k) = e_G
    m := φ(k) = F(k, k)
    φ⁻¹(y)    = F(k, F(F(k, y), k))              = m ⋆ inv(m ⋆ inv(φ⁻¹ y))
    x ⋆ inv y = F(φ⁻¹(x), φ(y))
    e_G       = x ⋆ inv x,   inv y = e_G ⋆ inv y,   x ⋆ y = x ⋆ inv(inv y).

Proof is the displayed computation; it uses φ⁻¹∘φ = id once (third line)
and φ∘φ⁻¹ = id once (fourth line). Checked numerically for four
instances in `eml_bases.py` §2.

**EML is the instance** G = (ℂ, +), φ = exp, k = exp(0) = 1, m = e. The
lemma delivers +, −, exp, ln, 0, 1, e. The rest is the classical exp-log
closure: x·y = exp(ln x + ln y), 1/x = exp(−ln x), x^y = exp(y ln x),
√x = x^{1/2}, −1 = 0 − 1, iπ = ln(−1), π = √(−(iπ)²), i = (−1)^{1/2},
sin/cos by Euler's formula. All of these words are built and checked in
§3c of the script; their sizes:

| word | F-nodes | | word | F-nodes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| e | 1 | | x + y | 13 |
| exp x | 1 | | x · y | 20 |
| ln x | 3 | | 1/x | 12 |
| 0 | 3 | | x / y | 40 |
| x − y | 5 | | √x | 43 |
| −x | 8 | | π | 54 |
| −1 | 8 | | sin x | 240 |

(The paper's own words may be shorter; these are the lemma's words,
built mechanically.)

So the theorem decomposes into two parts of very different character:

- **(A) algebraic, general:** any abelian group plus any bijection can be
  packaged into one binary operation with one constant — the Sheffer /
  NAND move, where NAND = AND (mixing) + NOT (inverse), and EML =
  exp (mixing, since it carries + to ×) + ln (its inverse) + − (group
  inverse).
- **(B) analytic, classical:** ⟨+, −, exp, ln⟩ over ℂ generates the EL
  class (Liouville's exp-log towers).

The surprise in the paper is (A) — that it is this cheap — not (B). And
it explains the constant: **1 is exp(0), the image of the group identity**.
With constant e instead, the closure search finds none of the basic
targets at depth ≤ 4 (§3 table): `eml(x, e) = e^x − 1`, not e^x.

## 2. Other bases

### 2a. The lemma's family

Every choice of (G, ⋆, φ) with elementary φ and φ⁻¹ gives a
one-operator, one-constant packaging of ⟨⋆, inv, φ, φ⁻¹⟩. Universality
for the EL class then reduces to one question: **does ⟨⋆, inv, φ, φ⁻¹, k⟩
contain exp and ×?**

| (G, ⋆) | φ | F(x, y) | constant | generates | EL-universal? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (ℂ, +) | exp | exp(x) − Log(y) | 1 | +, −, exp, ln | **yes** (EML) |
| (ℂ*, ×) | exp | exp(x) / Log(y) | e | ×, ÷, exp, ln; then + = ln(e^x e^y) | **yes on a region** (EDL, new here) |
| (ℝ, +) | sinh | sinh(x) − asinh(y) | 0 | +, −, sinh, asinh | **no** (parity, 2b) |
| (ℝ, +) | x³ | x³ − ∛y | 0 | +, −, cubes, cube roots | no (algebraic) |
| (ℂ, +) | Log | Log(x) − exp(y) | Log(0) = −∞ | lemma does not apply | open |

Notes on the two interesting rows.

- **EDL = exp(x)/Log(y), constant e.** The multiplicative twin of EML.
  Its words are valid on a much thinner region than EML's: the ln word
  `F(e, F(F(e, y), e)) = e^e / Log(exp(e^e / Log y))` needs
  Im(e^e / Log y) ∈ (−π, π], which fails for most complex y and even for
  real y near 1, whereas EML's ln word needs only Im(e − Log y) = −Arg y
  ∈ (−π, π], which fails only on the negative real axis. Subtraction
  keeps imaginary parts bounded; division does not. Also the constant 1
  is a pole of the right argument (Log 1 = 0). Verified in §2/§3 of the
  script on a near-real region; the generic-region search does not find
  its ln word because the region violates the strip condition.
- **The mirror Log(x) − exp(y).** Not trivially equivalent to EML: every
  term is a leaf or `Log(A) − exp(B)`, so `ln x` itself is never produced
  literally (exp(B) ≠ 0), and the lemma's constant φ(0) = Log 0 does not
  exist. The closure search from constant 1 finds none of the targets
  through depth 4. Whether it is universal from some constant is open
  here; I have neither a word for 0 nor an invariant excluding it.

### 2b. Necessary conditions, each with a proof

A single operator F with constant c generating the EL class must satisfy:

1. **Transcendence.** If F is algebraic, the closure is algebraic (no exp).
2. **Exponential growth.** The closure of `x − Log(y)` from 1 satisfies
   |T(x)| ≤ C_T (1 + |x|) by induction (|T| ≤ |T₁| + ln|T₂| + π), so exp is
   unreachable. Some subterm of F must grow super-polynomially.
3. **Multivaluedness.** If F is single-valued with isolated singularities
   (e.g. `exp(x) − y`), every generated function is single-valued, so Log
   and √ are unreachable. F must contain a branch point.
4. **An inverse.** `exp(x) + Log(y)` from constant 1 maps (1, ∞)² into
   (1, ∞) (checked on 2000+ generated functions), so no negative constant,
   no −x, no x − y, no i, no π. `sinh(x) − asinh(y)` from 0: every generated
   f satisfies f(−x, −y) = −f(x, y) (leaves 0, x, y are odd; sinh, asinh
   are odd; − preserves it), so exp, x·y and every nonzero constant are
   unreachable.

EML is the smallest combination that passes all four: one function of
exponential growth, its multivalued inverse, and a group inverse. The
argument swaps and sign flips (`ln(x) − exp(y)`, `exp(x) − ln(y) − 1`)
change which constants are available, which is why they behave
differently at low depth.

### 2d. Universal algebra placement

This is the continuous version of a Sheffer function. What the packaging
lemma says in clone language: a clone that contains an abelian group
operation and a bijection with its inverse is generated, together with
one constant, by a single binary operation. I state it only in that form;
I have not checked the general clone-theory literature for the sharpest
known statement.

## 3. Eigen-frames, in the sense of arithmetic/0023–0024

That workstream's criterion: an eigen frame is a canonical form that is
the common eigenbasis of an operator family definable from the necessary
generators; distinct families fail to commute; concentration trades. The
same structure exists here, and it is older than the analogy.

### 3a. Differentiation is triangular in the exp/log tower: exp is the eigen part, log is the Jordan part

Write an EL function inside a tower K₀ = ℂ(x) ⊂ K₁ ⊂ … ⊂ Kₙ, each step
adjoining θ = exp(u) or θ = log(u) with u in the previous field (every
EML tree spells such a tower: each node contributes one exp extension by
its left subtree and one log extension by its right subtree; §4b of the
script prints them). The derivation D = d/dx acts on the generators as

    D exp(u) = u' · exp(u)        eigenvector, eigenvalue u'
    D log(u) = u'/u ∈ K_{i−1}     Jordan chain: D sends the top down a level.

So **EML's two arguments are precisely the eigen part and the Jordan part
of differentiation.** Liouville's theorem is the statement that D's
cokernel is spanned by the Jordan tops: ∫f is elementary iff
f = v₀' + Σ cᵢ vᵢ'/vᵢ with vᵢ in the field and cᵢ constants — the only
way to leave the field is through new logarithms.

### 3b. The additive and multiplicative frames, and exp as the intertwiner

Two commuting families with their eigenbases:

| frame | operator | eigenbasis | transform | Jordan chain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| additive | D = d/dt | e^{λt} | Fourier / Laplace | polynomials tⁿ/n! (D nilpotent) |
| multiplicative | θ = x d/dx | x^λ | Mellin | (log x)ⁿ/n! (θ nilpotent) |

They are conjugate: **D(g ∘ exp) = (θg) ∘ exp** (verified symbolically,
script §5). exp carries + to × and D to θ; ln carries them back. EML
packages the intertwiner and its inverse with the additive group — it is
the operator that moves between the two frames. Concentration trades in
the 0024 sense: e^x is one term in the additive frame and an infinite
series in the power frame; xⁿ the reverse. I record this as an
observation with no inequality attached.

### 3c. Where convexity lives: the function level is a theorem, the constant level a conjecture

Semantic convexity (arithmetic/0001) needs a knowably terminating
reduction to a unique canonical form, with "reduces to 0 iff true".

- **Function level.** In a tower, an element has a canonical
  representation (reduced rational function in the top generator with
  coefficients below), and the Risch structure theorem — which rests on
  Ax's theorem, the function-field form of Schanuel — decides whether a
  new exp or log is algebraic over the tower and, if so, exhibits the
  relation (it is always a product of powers of existing generators and
  constants). So "two EML trees denote the same function" is decidable
  **relative to the constant field**.
- **Constant level.** The constants of EML trees are the EL numbers
  (variable-free trees). Deciding whether an EL number is zero is the
  exp-log constant problem: Richardson's procedure terminates on every
  input iff Schanuel's conjecture holds; unconditionally, "≠ 0" is
  semi-decidable by evaluation because EL numbers are computable
  (Carney), and "= 0" has no known certificate.

This is the exact analogue of the arithmetic workstream's level
discipline (0034/0035: "the count level is a second copy of the layer's
language"): **the constant field of the EML function language is the
EML language itself, one level down**, and convexity of the upper level
reduces to convexity of the lower. Ax makes the upper level's own
relations trivial; Schanuel would make the lower level's.

A polarity remark. The repo's judgment certifies "= 0" (TRUE) by
rewriting and leaves the rest undecided. For EL constants the
unconditional one-sided judgment has the **opposite polarity**: "≠ 0" is
certified (compute to enough digits), "= 0" stays undecided. Under
Schanuel both sides close.

### 3d. Frame freedom

The tower is not unique: generator order, and the choice of which
subexpressions become generators, are frame parameters, like variable
order for the automaton frame in 0018. The structure theorem resolves
dependencies (e^x · e^x versus e^{2x}) within a chosen tower.

## 4. A semantically convex integration procedure

### 4a. Differentiation is a rewrite on EML trees

    d/dx eml(u, v) = eml(u, 1) · u' − v' / v

verified on six trees (script §4a). Products and quotients are
EML-expressible, so the EML class is closed under D by a term rewrite.
Differentiation is the cheap direction.

### 4b. Integration is Risch on the tower the tree spells out

Pipeline: EML tree → list of exp/log extensions (mechanical, §4b) → Risch
in that tower. Output is either an antiderivative in the tower plus new
logarithms with algebraic constants, or a proof of non-elementarity.
sympy's `risch_integrate` on sample trees:

| tree | function | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| eml(x, 1) | e^x | e^x |
| eml(x, x) | e^x − ln x | e^x − x ln x + x |
| eml(1, x) | e − ln x | e·x − x ln x + x |
| eml(x, eml(x, 1)) | e^x − x | e^x − x²/2 |
| eml(eml(x, 1), 1) | e^{e^x} | non-elementary |
| eml(1, eml(1, x)) | e − log(e − log x) | non-elementary |
| eml(x, 1)/x | e^x / x | non-elementary |
| 1/eml(1, x) | 1/(e − ln x) | non-elementary |

### 4c. The convexity audit

Against the three clauses of arithmetic/0001:

1. **Knowable termination.** Risch in the transcendental (exp/log) case
   is an algorithm whose bounds are read off degrees in the tower. Yes.
2. **Unique canonical form.** The antiderivative is unique up to a
   constant given the tower; the certificate `D(G) − f → 0` is checked by
   rewriting into the tower's canonical form. Yes, relative to the frame.
3. **Semantic completeness.** Requires a zero-test on the constant field.
   For EML constants that is the EL zero problem (§3c): conditional on
   Schanuel.

So the honest statement: **integration on EML trees is semantically
convex relative to a constant-field oracle; unconditionally it is convex
in the "≠ 0"-certifying polarity; under Schanuel it is convex outright.**
The shape matches the arithmetic workstream's central finding: the
derivative is a rewrite (cheap), the integral is the change of basis
(expensive; it is a search for a certificate that differentiation then
checks). This is the same `KH ^ H` pattern — a hypothesis G is verified by
reducing `D(G) − f` to 0 — with Risch as the search that guarantees the
certificate is found when it exists.

### 4d. The class is not closed under elementary integration

Liouville's theorem permits constants cᵢ algebraic over the base field.
For f = 1/(x⁵ − x − 1) (Galois group S₅), ∫f = Σ_α cₐ ln(x − α) over the
five roots. The integrand is EL; the antiderivative is elementary in
Ritt's sense but needs the roots α. Whether it has some other EML
expression I cannot exclude; Chow conjectures that some algebraic numbers
are not EL, and the stylewarning monodromy argument is about the generic
root as a function of coefficients, not this fixed one. **Plausible, not
proved: EL is not closed under elementary integration.** This is the
place where "elementary" (Ritt) and "EML-expressible" (EL) come apart
inside the integration problem itself.

## 5. Branch cuts: identities of germs, not of functions on ℂ

- The reported ln word `eml(1, eml(eml(1, x), 1))` equals Log x off the
  negative real axis and equals **Log x − 2πi on it** (exact: Im(e − Log y)
  = −Arg y = −π when Arg y = π, and Log(exp w) = w only for
  Im w ∈ (−π, π]). Exact arithmetic (sympy) and 50-digit mpmath both give
  ln(−1) = −iπ from the word.
- **Double precision hides the defect**: exp(e − iπ) rounds to
  −e^e − 1.9·10⁻¹⁵ i, which lands the intermediate on the −π side of the
  cut and flips the branch back, so float64 returns +iπ. A calculator
  implementation would look correct there by luck.
- Consequence: every EML word for a multivalued target is an identity of
  analytic germs (equivalently, an identity in the differential field), not
  of functions on ℂ. That is the standard convention of the Risch
  literature, and it is the right reading of the paper's theorem. Whether
  some EML word equals x − y at **every** point of ℂ² is open here: y can
  enter a term only through exp(y) (loses Im y mod 2π) or Log(y) (fine, but
  then must be undone by an exp higher up); I found neither a uniform word
  nor an impossibility proof.

## 6. Implications, collected

1. The result is an algebraic packaging fact (§1) sitting on classical
   analysis (§1B). Its genuine content is that the packaging costs one
   constant and one node type; its practical value is the uniform node
   (trainable circuits, symbolic regression, the universal-approximation
   follow-up).
2. There is a whole family of such bases (§2a), of which EML is the
   additive-group member and EDL the multiplicative one; the four
   necessary conditions (§2b) explain why the search found what it found.
3. The eigen-frame reading is literal (§3): exp-generators are eigenvectors
   of differentiation, log-generators its Jordan chains; exp intertwines
   the additive and multiplicative frames; EML is that intertwiner
   packaged with its inverse.
4. Convex integration exists and is called Risch (§4); its convexity
   splits exactly along the level discipline this repository already uses,
   with Ax's theorem closing the function level and Schanuel's conjecture
   the constant level.
5. The one-sided judgment for EL constants has the opposite polarity to
   the repository's (§3c).

## Open

- The mirror `Log(x) − exp(y)`: universal from some constant, or excluded
  by an invariant?
- Uniform (pointwise on ℂ²) EML words for subtraction: exist or not.
- A tighter node-count table: are the lemma's words minimal? (The paper
  reports shallow trees; my `x − y` at 5 nodes is likely the paper's.)
- EL not closed under elementary integration (§4d): prove it for
  ∫dx/(x⁵ − x − 1), which needs "the roots of x⁵ − x − 1 are not EL
  numbers" — an instance of Chow's conjecture.
- An uncertainty inequality between the additive and multiplicative frames
  for EL functions (the 0024 program, one level up).
