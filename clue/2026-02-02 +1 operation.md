$(A) has the property that it is a series of 1s until the first 0, then 0s thereafter.

A + 1 needs to handle carry through every trailing 1 in the number, where we 0 out all trailing 1s then make the first 0 a 1.

This means that A + 1 := A ^ inc($A) ^ 1

```
A ^ inc($A) ^ 1 examples

Assume reverse(A) :=          1000000
reverse(inc($A)) =            0100000
reverse(A ^ inc($A) ^ 1) =    0100000

Assume reverse(A) :=          0100000
reverse(inc($A)) =            0000000
reverse(A ^ inc($A) ^ 1) =    1100000

Assume reverse(A) :=          0000000
reverse(inc($A)) =            0000000
reverse(A ^ inc($A) ^ 1) =    1000000

Assume reverse(A) :=          1111010
reverse(inc($A)) =            0111100
reverse(A ^ inc($A) ^ 1) =    0000110
```

Importantly, `inc($A) != $inc(A)`. In general, `$inc(A)` is trivially true.

# Definition of +1

let
    `n1(x) := 2x + 1`
    `n0(x) := 2x`
    `[&n1](x) := (x)&(2x + 1)&(4x + 3)&...` i.e. 1 for every contiguous leading 1, zero everywhere else; $x
    `[^n0](x) := x ^ 2x ^ 4x ^ ...` i.e. !x

Then +1 zeros out every leading 1, turning the first 0 to a 1.

```
x
^ [&n1](x)   -- cancel every leading 1
^ ([&n1](x) ^ n0([&n1](x)) ^ 1)     -- flip the first zero after leading 1s to a 1
```

which expands to `x ^ n0([&n1](x)) ^ 1`

x+2 is then
```
x ^ n0([&n1](x)) ^ 1
^ n0([&n1](x ^ n0([&n1](x)) ^ 1))
^ 1
```

Focusing on `n0([&n1](x ^ n0([&n1](x)) ^ 1))`, the term overall is 0 for odd x (x+1 has a 0 in the ones place -> `[&n1](x+1) == 0`). The internal term `n0([&n1](x))` is 0 for even x. Therefore, the internal term `n0([&n1](x))` never has an effect on the overall result, and we can write the entire expression taking x -> x+2 as

`x ^ n0([&n1](x)) ^ n0([&n1](x ^ 1))`

Then repeating the same process, the expression taking x -> x+3 follows the same pattern:

`x ^ 1 ^ n0([&n1](x)) ^ n0([&n1](x ^ 1)) ^ n0([&n1](x ^ 2))`

Overall, for the expression taking `x -> x+a` the `x` term always appears, the 1 term appears for odd additions, and beyond that we take the ^-total of `n0([&n1](x ^ b))` where b ranges over `[0, a-1]`
