# 0024 — The eigenbases classify, and the classification already has a name

Proceeding down 0023's queue: classify the eigenbases, audit
convexity-compatibility, and get an exact uncertainty theorem in hand.
All three have landed, the first with a surprise ending. Machine
checks: `output/eigenbasis_classification.py`.

## 1. The classification closes at one coordinate

The function space of a single variable is GF(2)², which has **exactly
three unordered bases** — enumerable, enumerated:

| basis | diagonalises | traditional names |
|---|---|---|
| {1, x} | substitute-to-0 | ANF / **positive Davio** |
| {1, 1^x} | substitute-to-1 | dual / **negative Davio** |
| {x, 1^x} | multiply-by-g | minterm / **Shannon** |

There is no fourth. And the translation operator is diagonal in *none*
of them: **(T_a + I)² = 0 over GF(2)** (verified as matrices), so every
translation is unipotent, hence non-diagonalisable — the character
(Walsh) basis exists only after lifting coefficients to ℤ. **The
counting layer is forced** for that basis, the way complex numbers are
forced for spin.

## 2. The full taxonomy is the decision-diagram taxonomy

Products over coordinates now enumerate the frames: per variable,
choose one of the three measurements; then choose flat spectrum or
shared DAG (the quotient move); then an order; optionally the ℤ lift.
This is *exactly* the Kronecker decision-diagram taxonomy of logic
synthesis:

| frame | construction | literature name |
|---|---|---|
| ANF | flat, all positive-Davio | PPRM (Zhegalkin) |
| polarity forms | flat, Davio mix per variable | FPRM |
| truth table | flat, all Shannon | — |
| **minimal automaton / OBDD** | all Shannon **+ sharing** | OBDD (Bryant) |
| shared ANF | all positive-Davio + sharing | FDD (Kebschull et al.) |
| mixed + sharing | per-variable choice + sharing | KFDD (Drechsler et al.) |
| lifted | ℤ coefficients | Walsh spectra / *BMD |

Two consequences worth stating plainly:

- **The sentence/automaton dichotomy dissolves into coordinates of a
  single taxonomy**: the automaton is the Shannon choice *plus the
  sharing (quotient) move*; the ANF is the Davio choice, flat. Our
  measured ANF-versus-automaton incomparability (0019) is an instance
  of the *known* exponential OBDD/FDD separations, in both directions,
  from that literature.
- **The taxonomy is generated by exactly the framework's own moves**:
  the three per-coordinate measurements are the eigenbases of
  substitute-0 / substitute-1 / multiply — all wiring-definable — the
  sharing move is Myhill–Nerode quotienting (0006), the order is the
  frame freedom (0018), the lift is the counting layer. Nothing
  imported; the classification is internal.

## 3. Frames move blind spots; they do not remove them

**Polarity cures the refutation event.** "At least one of n" costs
2ⁿ−1 terms at polarity 0 and **exactly 2 terms** at the
all-complemented polarity, with the exact interpolation 2^(n−|p|) + 1
(verified, n = 10: 1023 → 257 → 33 → 5 → 2). The fixed-polarity form
with polarity p is precisely the ANF of f(x ^ p) — verified by
reconstruction — so **the translations, which have no eigenbasis of
their own, act as frame changes on the ANF family**. (QM parallel:
momentum generates translations of the position frame.)

But the blind spot moves rather than vanishing: parity keeps ~n terms
at *every* polarity (translation fixes its top monomial). And the
counting argument of 0022 stands above all frames: with 3ⁿ · orders ·
polarities frames of description length ≤ s covering at most
~2^(s+O(n log n)) statements each, almost every statement is
exponential in **every** frame of the taxonomy. Explicit such
statements are lower-bound territory, as expected.

## 4. Convexity-compatibility: the audit is uniform

Every frame in the table is canonical given its parameters (basis
choice, order, polarity) and reached by a terminating transform
(butterflies for the flat forms; minimise for the shared forms) —
**every frame is semantically convex**. Convexity does not distinguish
between frames; it is the property of the *taxonomy*, not of a
member. What distinguishes members is concentration, which is
statement-relative. (Choosing the best frame is itself hard — optimal
OBDD ordering and optimal FPRM polarity are both known NP-hard
optimisation problems — so "work in the best frame" is not a free
algorithmic move.)

## 5. The first exact uncertainty theorem, in hand

For the minterm × Walsh pair, the Donoho–Stark inequality is exact:

    support(f) · support(Walsh f) ≥ 2ⁿ

Verified on 300 random sparse indicators (n = 8, minimum observed
product exactly 256 = 2ⁿ), and **tight precisely on subspace
indicators** — 1×256, 4×64, 16×16, 64×4, 256×1 — the coherent states
of this basis pair. So the uncertainty program has its first exact
theorem, and its model: find the analogue for the ANF × automaton
pair, whose extremals (at-least-one on one side, windowed parity on
the other) are already measured.

## Open, sharpened

- **ANF × automaton uncertainty inequality**: does size(ANF) ·
  size(OBDD) admit a lower bound in terms of some invariant of the
  statement (the DS analogue)? The two extremal families are in hand.
- **Explicit everywhere-spread statements**: exist by counting in
  every frame simultaneously; an explicit family would be a
  representation-independent hardness result (and is expected to be
  hard to exhibit for exactly that reason).
- **Affine extension**: GL(n,2) input transforms conjugate the
  restriction family — linearly-transformed frames (LTBDDs in the DD
  literature). Whether the affine orbit adds essentially new
  concentration classes or only rotates the existing ones is
  unexplored here.
