## 1. Completeness at one coordinate — a machine theorem, with a machine-caught correction

An eigenframe diagonalises a commuting family generated by the
logic's own primitives — and at one coordinate the primitive-
generated operator monoid is *finite* and can be enumerated outright.

**Over GF(2)** (all 16 linear maps of GF(2)²): every operator with
two distinct eigenvalues has its eigenbasis among the three known
decompositions — Shannon, positive Davio, negative Davio — and all
three are realised. Translation is unipotent (repeated eigenvalue),
which is what forces the counting lift for Walsh (0023/0024).

**Over the rationals** (the counting lift): the projective closure of
the monoid generated by {substitute-0, substitute-1, translate,
multiply-by-x, multiply-by-(1−x), sum} has 11 elements. My clean
claim "only four eigenbases arise" was **false — the machine caught
it**: cross-layer composites force *hybrid* bases (multiply-x after
sum has eigenbasis {x, 1−2x}). The repaired theorem is stronger:

> **The eigenvector pool is finite and closed.** Every member of the
> closure with two distinct eigenvalues has both eigenvectors in the
> four-element pool {1, x, 1−x, 1−2x}.

Hence at most C(4,2) = 6 per-coordinate kinds over the lift: the four
pure bases (point, moment/*BMD, negated moment, Walsh — each the
eigenbasis of a single primitive family) plus the two realised
hybrids {x, 1−2x} and {1−x, 1−2x} — which are exactly the hybrid
decision-diagram rows of the literature (Clarke–Fujita–Zhao), forced
only by composites. Infinitely many bases exist over ℚ; the logic's
operators only ever draw from the pool. Corrected count: 3 GF(2)
kinds + ≤ 6 lift kinds, × {flat, shared}, × the parameter groups.

**Relative-completeness assumptions, stated** (the conjecture's
honest residue — a refuting frame must break one): (i) coordinate-
product structure up to the parameter groups (order, polarity,
GL(n,2) — itself generated by hidden channels plus ^); (ii) operator
inventory = the logic's finite basis (0012/0013); (iii) sharing as
the one quotient move (0027).

## 2. The cure exhibits: the extended parameters are not optional

Two succinct, polynomial-time-decidable families escape **all seven
measured frames simultaneously** — and each lands exactly in one
conjectured extension kind:

**Random affine system** (d = 8 parities, n = 16): models 256,
ANF 12304, dual 3435, Walsh 256, OBDD 225, FDD 314, negFDD 510 —
every frame at the 2^(n/2) scale — yet Gaussian elimination decides
it in polynomial time. Completing the constraint matrix to
L ∈ GL(n,2) and presenting the same statement in coordinates z = Lx
gives a 33-state OBDD (linear). **Gaussian elimination is the
GL(n,2) parameter's canonical algorithm.**

**Multiplication** (middle bit, operand width k): OBDD 126→280→634,
FDD 72→142→328, negFDD 85→191→488, ANF 26→78→288 for k = 5, 6, 7 —
exponential growth in every bit-level frame measured (and OBDD
hardness in every order is Bryant's theorem) — while the word-level
weight-normalised moment diagram (*BMD, implemented here) is linear:
28→34→40. **Word arithmetic is the moment lift's canonical
algorithm.**

Had the frame list been closed at the seven, the escape-union
argument would be *refuted* by these two families ("escapes all
frames" would not imply "hard"). Instead each is absorbed by a
conjectured kind — the sharpest evidence that the finiteness
conjecture with its parameter groups is the right closure, and the
demonstration that **completeness is the load-bearing step**.

## 3. The subclass × frame map

The refined object the coarse matrix approximates (measured; see the
figure):

    subclass         representative      home frames          canonical algorithm
    counting         majority            OBDD, FDD, negFDD    dynamic counting (DP)
    sparse-model     16 deals            minterm, OBDD        enumerate / sweep
    parity-spread    windowed parity     ANF, dual, FDD, negFDD  expand-and-cancel
    selector         one-hot mux         OBDD, negFDD, dual   branch and trace
    affine system    8 parities          GL-OBDD only         Gaussian elimination
    word arithmetic  x·y middle bit      *BMD only            schoolbook arithmetic
    generic          random statement    NONE (counting bound) — and not succinct

Notable measurements: sharing cures counting (all three shared
frames hold majority at ~n² while every flat frame escapes); the
selector's home includes **dual ANF** (24 terms — the co-singleton
structure; dual-small forces negFDD-small by the mirrored fiber
law); the sparse-model class escapes *both* Davio sharings by the
survivor law while staying in minterm and OBDD.

**The directed escape graph**: each subclass sits inside its home
frames' subexponential islands; every flow-matrix edge leaving the
home set is an exponential escape at that cell's stated rate. The
exact laws are the frame-free invariants that *define* the
subclasses: straddle structure (parity-spread), suffix-sets
(sparse-model), fiber shattering (selector), weight-dependence
(counting), coset structure (affine), carry structure (word
arithmetic). And every classical polynomial algorithm family met so
far — DP, enumeration, cancellation, branching, Gauss, word
arithmetic — **is some frame's canonicalisation**. No known
polynomial algorithm for this problem class is currently frameless.

## 4. The proof sketch, audited step by step

- **Step 1, "finitely many eigenframes"** — machine-supported at one
  coordinate (§1), relative to three stated assumptions; the
  parameter groups are mandatory (§2).
- **Step 2, "best possible = the N-frame portfolio"** — the
  load-bearing open step. It is the claim that the frame portfolio
  is an *optimal* algorithm family — the analogue of the
  optimal-proof-system question (Krajíček–Pudlák, open since 1989).
  Pressure from both sides: 0020's B1 (PPSZ decides without visiting
  any canonical form) against it; the frame-shadowing of every known
  algorithm family (§3) for it. Falsifiable form: exhibit a succinct
  poly-time family exponential in every frame of the *completed*
  list, parameters included. None is known here.
- **Step 3, "the union of subexponential islands is strict"** — a
  theorem at the representation level (counting), but the counting
  witnesses are not succinct, and instances are sentences. For
  succinct inputs, strictness is exactly the conclusion and cannot
  be assumed.
- **Step 4, "therefore P ≠ NP"** — follows from 2 + succinct-3. The
  program's real content: (a) close the frame list, (b) prove every
  algorithm is frame-shadowed. The proven CSP dichotomy
  (Bulatov/Zhuk 2017) is the exact *shape* of the conclusion —
  finitely many tractable islands, each algebraically characterised,
  everything else NP-hard — and its islands line up with frames
  (affine ↔ GL/Davio, majority-closed ↔ shared Shannon, semilattice
  ↔ the order fragment). Its engine is the polymorphism/Pol–Inv
  machinery this thread met in 0013 — proved through algebra, not
  representation size. That is the strongest hint about where
  Step 2's bridge should be looked for: **polymorphisms ↔ frames**,
  not size bounds alone.
