
# 0040 — The tiles are windows, and the N-layer is the automaton

0039 §5.1 asked whether the realisable non-emptiness vectors are
generated by the single law `N(sᵏx) = N(sᵏ⁺¹x)`. Chased. The answer is
**no**, and the reason identifies the whole structure. Code:
`output/tiles_are_windows.py`.

---

## 1. Tiles are windows

Over the symbols `{x, s(x), …, sⁿ⁻¹(x)}`, the tile of polarity `p` is
non-empty **exactly when `p` occurs as a length-n window**
`(xᵢ, xᵢ₋₁, …, xᵢ₋ₙ₊₁)` of x's bit string. Verified for `n = 2, 3, 4`
over every `x < 2¹²`.

That is the whole content of the tile picture, and it is sharper than
"circles on a Venn diagram". A tile is not an arbitrary region — **it is
the assertion that a particular bit pattern appears somewhere in `x`.**

The reason is immediate once seen: bit `i` of `sᵏ(x)` is bit `i−k` of
`x`, so fixing the polarity of every `sᵏ(x)` at position `i` fixes the
window of `x` ending at `i`.

## 2. So the single law is nowhere near sufficient

| n | realisable | satisfying `N(sᵏx) = N(sᵏ⁺¹x)` | of `2^(2ⁿ)` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | **3** | 12 | 16 |
| 3 | **11** | 220 | 256 |
| 4 | **57** | 64596 | 65536 |

The law is **sound** — every realisable vector satisfies it, checked —
and it permits vastly more than occurs. At `n = 4` it excludes under 1%
of vectors while reality excludes 99.9%.

So the hoped-for cheap answer of 0039 is refuted: the N-layer's
constraints are *not* as cheap as the tiles are expensive.

## 3. What does characterise it: the de Bruijn condition

Consecutive windows overlap in `n−1` symbols. So the windows occurring
in `x` are exactly the vertices visited by a **sliding-window walk** —
free bits while reading `x`, then forced zeros above its top bit.

```
n   realisable   de Bruijn walk vertex-sets   equal
2            3                            3   True
3           11                           11   True
4           57                           57   True
```

Exactly equal at every `n` measured. The realisable set is the set of
**walk-vertex-sets in the de Bruijn graph of order `n−1`**, with the
zero-padding fixing the start and end.

## 4. What this settles

**The tiles are de Bruijn states, and the N-layer is the automaton.**

- `2ⁿ` tiles are `2ⁿ` de Bruijn vertices.
- The realisable set is the set of runs.
- "Which tiles are non-empty" is "which states the run visits".

So the tile decomposition does not replace the automaton frame — **it
reconstructs it**, at the same cost, from the sentence side. That
settles 0039 §5.2 as well: the pair (tile support, realisable set) is
canonical exactly insofar as the automaton is, and for the same reason,
because it *is* the automaton's run structure.

It also revises 0038's reading. There the sentence frame and the
automaton frame looked like two independent closed forms of one series,
peers with a measured incomparability (0019). At the level of *tiles*
they are not two forms of one thing — they are one thing. The
incomparability of 0019 is about how compactly a statement can be
*written* in each frame; it is not a difference in what the frames are.

## 5. The chain, end to end

Worth stating as one line, since it took five files:

> The corpus's containment test is complete exactly on the pointwise
> fragment (0037). `<<` is not pointwise, so the knowledge must be
> closed under it; the closure is an infinite union (0037) whose closed
> form is the nonemptiness indicator `N` (0038); the closure distributes
> over tiles, and over the shift-generated symbols the tiles are the
> windows of the bit string (0039, 0040) — so the closed knowledge is
> the run structure of the sliding-window automaton, and the sentence
> frame reaches the automaton by its own road.

That is the sentence-side derivation of 0023's "what forces the DFA",
now with the object identified rather than gestured at.

## 6. Open

1. **Does the identification survive more operators?** The window
   reading is specific to `s`. With `+` or `T` among the symbols the
   tiles are still Venn regions but the "window" reading has no obvious
   analogue, and the realisable set is whatever it is. Measuring it for
   `{x, x+1}` and `{x, T(x)}` is the immediate next experiment, and it
   would say whether "tiles are windows" is a fact about the shift or
   about the framework.
2. **The measure.** `|.|` is not a set-to-set map, so it has no tile of
   its own. Whether the counted tier's register can be read as a
   coarser quotient of the same run structure — counts of visits rather
   than sets of visited states — is the sharpest remaining question, and
   it is exactly the Parikh image of the run. That is not a coincidence:
   the counted tier's decision procedure (0034) *is* Parikh's theorem.
3. **Growth of the realisable set.** 3, 11, 57 at `n = 2, 3, 4`. Whether
   this is a known sequence for de Bruijn walk-sets would say how much
   the N-layer actually costs.
