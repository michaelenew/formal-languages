# 0049 — Containment closed the way the series were, and where the wall really is

0048 drove the sentence form's residue down to containment and stopped,
calling it a wall. That was premature, and it repeated a mistake this
workstream already got past once: when the base algebra could not reach
carry propagation, the answer was not "the algebra is too weak" but
*write the infinite series and name its closed form* — which is where
`T`, `U`, `!` and `N` came from (0042 §1).

Containment gets the same treatment here, and it works. What is left
afterwards is not about containment at all. Code:
`output/containment_closure.py`.

---

## 1. The infinite expression was already written down

0042 §2's **first** universal law is the fixpoint,

```
S(t)  =  t  ∘  σ(S t)
```

whose unfoldings are the partial sums

```
T(t) = t & bt & … & b^{k-1}t & b^k(T t)
U(t) = t | at | … | a^{k-1}t | a^k(U t)
```

and **every unfolding is a containment statement about the closed
form**: `T(t) ⊑ b^k(T t)` and `a^k(U t) ⊑ U(t)`, at every `k`. That is
the `1/2 + 1/4 + …` step — the partial sums are the finite unfoldings,
the series is the closed form, and the containment is what the limit
inherits.

0048 implemented telescoping (cancellation) and `collect` (combination)
and **never implemented the fixpoint (expansion)**, which is why
containment looked like an outside fact. It is not. No new primitive is
introduced anywhere in this file.

## 2. The name: the down-set, which is `^`-closed

The obstacle to reasoning about order in this algebra is that `^` is not
monotone. Measured:

```
a  U  T  N          monotone
!  lowset  lowzero  NOT monotone
```

`!(1) = Ω` and `!(3) = 1`, so `1 ⊑ 3` and `!(1) ⋢ !(3)`. That is the
third appearance of 0045's Post split.

The way across is not monotonicity but the **down-set**

```
↓S  =  { A : A ⊑ S }
```

which is closed under all three joins — `^` included, because a down-set
is a *subgroup* as well as a sublattice. 1800 closure checks, none
escape. So containment crosses `!` even though `!` is not monotone:

> every partial sum `⊕_{k<n} a^k(t)` lies in `↓U(t)`, and `↓U(t)` is
> `^`-closed, so the closed form `!(t)` lies in it too.

Which makes `U(t)` **the top of the subalgebra generated by the orbit
`{a^k(t)}`** — the statement 0048 §6 could only measure.

## 3. What the generators reach

All eleven containments 0048 needed, from the fixpoint law and the
down-set alone:

| | from |
|---|---|
| `T(t) ⊑ t`, `t ⊑ U(t)` | fixpoint, one unfolding |
| `T(t) ⊑ b(T t)`, `a(U t) ⊑ U(t)` | fixpoint, the tail |
| `!(t) ⊑ U(t)`, `a(!t) ⊑ U(t)`, `a(T t) ⊑ U(t)`, `T(t) ⊑ U(t)` | the infinite series into the `^`-closed down-set |
| `lowset(t) ⊑ t`, `lowzero(t) ⊑ t ^ Ω` | measure under its series |
| `t ⊑ N(t)` | `N` is the top of what it guards |

Sound: of 144 derived containments checked against the decision
procedure, **none is false**.

## 4. The closure question, measured

952 true containments among 2500 random pairs; **804 derived, 84%**. The
148 escapes classify:

```
118   reached once both sides are normalised -- not containment gaps,
        just unreduced terms
  7   the upper bound is an `N`, so it holds only where the guard is Ω
 23   genuinely outside the generators
```

and the 23 have one shape. The upper bound is a XOR-sum that is
*secretly a union or a complement*:

```
a(x)  ⊑  Ω ^ 1              (a complement)
1     ⊑  a(x) ^ 1           (a disjoint union)
1x    ⊑  a(x) ^ x ^ a(x)x   (which is x | a(x))
```

Adding the two clauses that recover them — `A ⊑ ¬C` iff `A & C = 0`, and
a monomial disjoint from the rest of a polynomial makes a join — is what
took the relation from 68% to 84%.

## 5. So: which joins have names

That escape family has an exact price. ANF stores `P | Q` as
`P ^ Q ^ PQ`, so an n-fold union expands by inclusion–exclusion:

| n | ANF monomials | if the join has a name |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 3 | 1 |
| 3 | 7 | 1 |
| 4 | 15 | 1 |
| 5 | 31 | 1 |
| 6 | 63 | 1 |
| 7 | 127 | 1 |

**The boundary is not containment and not `|`. It is which joins have
closed forms.** A named join is one atom and the fixpoint law reaches
every containment about it — `U(x) = x | a(U x)` folds the entire orbit.
An unnamed one is `2^n − 1` monomials with the join erased, and
recovering it is a search for a disjoint splitting. 0019(d) already put a
floor under that search: canonicalising this algebra is coNP-hard, so no
rule set recovers hidden joins in polynomial time unless P = NP.

That is the answer to "where does the boundary live as an expression".
It lives at **the gap between a join and its name**, and the schema is
the naming device.

## 6. Where the wall actually is: the rule shape, not the relation

Installing the generated relation into 0048's rewrite system moves the
unidentified pairs **19 → 19**. That is the most informative measurement
in the file.

`contain` has one shape — drop a factor from a monomial — and that needs
`A ⊑ B` between two *atoms* in the same product. The containment that
matters is against `b(T t)`, which is the *polynomial* `a(T t) ^ 1`:

```
T(t) ⊑ b(T t)   derived: yes    true: yes
T(t) ⊑ a(T t)   derived: no     true: no
```

so the factor-drop `T(t) & a(T t) → T(t)` is simply false, and the true
law is a polynomial identity,

```
T(t) & b(T t) = T(t)        i.e.       T(t)·a(T t) = T(t) ^ t&1
```

relating one two-monomial polynomial to another. **Nothing in the ANF
term is a "product of A and B" any more** — the product was expanded on
construction, exactly the way `|` was.

> ANF is a normal form for **one** of the three joins. The other two
> survive in it only as expansions, and any rule whose left-hand side is
> a product or a union has to re-find its own redex by search.

That is the multiplicative twin of §5's hidden-join problem, and it is
the wall: not expressive power, not containment, but that the
representation which makes `^` free makes `&` and `|` invisible.

## 7. Honest limits

- The 84% is over one random generator at depth 3, single variable. It
  measures reach, not completeness, and the relation is deliberately
  incomplete — it is a sound derivation system, not a decision
  procedure.
- Soundness is checked against 0047's decision procedure, which is
  itself verified rather than proved. Every containment asserted here is
  machine-checked; none is proved.
- §5's table is inclusion–exclusion on *independent* joinands. Overlapping
  ones collapse further — the orbit join `x | ax | … | a⁵x` is 3
  monomials, not 63 — which strengthens the point rather than weakening
  it, since that collapse is what having a name buys.
- §6 diagnoses the rule shape; it does **not** supply the repair. A
  product-aware rule shape has not been built or measured.
- The connection to 0019(d) is a citation of an established result in
  this corpus, not a re-derivation.

## 8. Open

1. **A representation that normalises the meet.** §6 says ANF is the
   wrong shape for two of the three joins. The natural candidate is to
   keep monomials as an antichain under `⊑` — now that §3 gives a usable
   `⊑` — so that `contain` is absorbed by construction the way
   commutativity is absorbed by ANF. Whether that survives contact with
   `^` is the question 0048 §8 asked and this file has now armed.
2. **Name more joins.** §5 makes naming the currency. The schema names
   the joins of orbits under one shift; `N = U | D` (0042 §3) is the
   only named join of two *different* cells. Which joins are worth
   names, and whether that family is finite, is the sharp form of "how
   far does the sentence form close".
3. **Is 84% a plateau or an artifact?** The 23 structural escapes are
   all hidden joins or complements. If a complete join-recovery
   procedure exists at exponential cost, the relation is complete and
   the wall is entirely about cost — which would put this workstream's
   boundary exactly on 0019(d)'s, and say the Gödel-flavoured obstacle
   here is complexity rather than expressiveness.
